Point your devices at your blocker

Only needed for the whole-network options (Pi-hole, AdGuard Home, Technitium, a DNS server). Browser and app options work on their own. You'll need your blocker's IP address, for example 192.168.1.2: it's shown on the blocker's dashboard and in your router's list of connected devices.

Router (covers every device at once, recommended)
  1. Open your router's settings page in a browser. The address and password are usually on a sticker on the router; common addresses are 192.168.1.1, 192.168.0.1 and 10.0.0.1.
  2. Find the DHCP or LAN settings (sometimes under Advanced or Network), and look for DNS server.
  3. Enter your blocker's IP address as the only DNS server and save. If it insists on a second one, enter the same address again.
  4. Restart your devices, or turn Wi-Fi off and on, so they pick up the change.

Every router is a little different. If you can't find the setting, search for your router's model with "change DNS server", check its manual, or ask your internet provider or the router's maker. Some provider-supplied routers don't allow it at all; then set DNS on each device below, or use your blocker's own DHCP server (Pi-hole and AdGuard Home both have one) and turn off the router's.

Windows 11
  1. Open Settings → Network & internet and click Wi-Fi or Ethernet, then your network (on Wi-Fi: Hardware properties).
  2. Next to DNS server assignment, click Edit, choose Manual and turn on IPv4.
  3. Enter your blocker's IP address as Preferred DNS, leave Alternate DNS empty, and click Save.
Mac
  1. Open System Settings → Network, click Wi-Fi (or Ethernet), then Details… next to your network.
  2. Open the DNS tab. Remove any servers listed with −, then click + and enter your blocker's IP address.
  3. Click OK.
iPhone and iPad
  1. Open Settings → Wi-Fi and tap the ⓘ next to your network.
  2. Tap Configure DNS → Manual. Delete the existing servers, tap Add Server and enter your blocker's IP address. Tap Save.
  3. This is per Wi-Fi network, and doesn't apply on mobile data.
Android
  1. Setting DNS on the router is much easier on Android: per-network DNS means switching the Wi-Fi to a fixed ("static") IP address, which is fiddly.
  2. Either way, check Settings → Network & internet → Private DNS is set to Off or Automatic. A named provider there (such as dns.google) skips your blocker entirely.
Linux
  1. In GNOME or KDE network settings, open your connection's IPv4 tab.
  2. Turn off automatic DNS and enter your blocker's IP address, then reconnect.
  3. From a terminal with NetworkManager: nmcli con mod "Your connection" ipv4.dns 192.168.1.2 ipv4.ignore-auto-dns yes, then nmcli con up "Your connection".

Make sure nothing goes around it

  • One DNS server only. Don't add a backup such as 8.8.8.8: devices use both, and anything looked up through the backup isn't blocked.
  • Browser "secure DNS". In Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox, a secure DNS setting that names a provider (Cloudflare, Google and so on) sends lookups straight there. Set it to Off or to use your current provider.
  • IPv6. Some routers also hand out their own IPv6 DNS server, which devices may use instead. If your blocker sees little traffic, set the router's IPv6 DNS to your blocker too, or turn that off.
  • Check it worked: browse a little on the device, then look for its lookups in your blocker's query log.
